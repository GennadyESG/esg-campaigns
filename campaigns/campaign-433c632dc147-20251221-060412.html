Australia’s ASIC releases climate reporting modules to help SMEs and suppliers comply

December 15, 2025

Australia’s regulator released the first three sustainability reporting education modules aimed at smaller companies and SME suppliers entering the new reporting regime. The materials explain Corporations Act climate disclosure basics and physical risk concepts, with more modules on transition risks, opportunities and emissions accounting due by early 2026. ASIC says the resources will support SMEs that prepare reports or supply larger reporting entities. SME owners can download the modules now to map risks, set governance, and prepare data and controls ahead of customer or lender requests.

EU deforestation rules delayed; micro and small operators get six-month cushion

December 18, 2025

EU governments approved a targeted revision to the deforestation‑free products law, streamlining due diligence and postponing application to December 30, 2026. Micro and small operators get an extra six months to comply, shifting their start date to June 30, 2027. Certain printed products were removed from scope, and a simplification review is due by April 30, 2026. SMEs in coffee, cocoa, rubber, palm oil, wood, soy or cattle supply chains should use the extra time to build traceability, collect geolocation proof, and prepare due diligence statements.

SM and GRI launch free e-learning to build MSME sustainability reporting skills

December 15, 2025

SM Investments and the Global Reporting Initiative unveiled a free online “bridge course” to help Philippine MSMEs start sustainability reporting. The course targets small suppliers and partners, offering simple training aligned with GRI and emerging ISSB expectations. SM will sponsor access while GRI handles distribution through partner platforms and SM’s MSME network. MSME owners can enroll to learn basic ESG concepts, benefits of reporting, and practical first steps to begin disclosures demanded by buyers and banks.

Korean buyers reward suppliers’ ESG performance; SMEs face rising evaluation stakes

December 15, 2025

A Korea Federation of SMEs review shows 45.4% of large and mid-sized listed companies now give incentives based on partner ESG evaluations. More buyers are linking contract terms to supplier ESG scores, reflecting stronger supply chain management. For SME suppliers, this means better pricing or access if ESG performance improves, and potential penalties if it lags. Owners should prepare for assessments by documenting safety, environmental controls, labor practices, and governance basics to win preferred-supplier status.
