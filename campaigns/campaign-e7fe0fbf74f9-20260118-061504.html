EU and EBRD unveil €76.75m guarantee to boost SME ESG lending

January 13, 2026

The European Commission and the European Bank for Reconstruction and Development signed a new guarantee of up to €76.75 million to help banks finance SMEs and mid‑caps that meet ESG criteria. The program targets firms in the Western Balkans, Eastern Neighbourhood, North Africa and the Middle East, Türkiye, Central Asia and Mongolia. It aims to accelerate greener and fairer value chains by making access to finance easier for small businesses investing in sustainability. SMEs in these regions can approach participating EBRD partner banks to seek ESG‑linked financing.

EIB backs Piraeus Bank to lend Greek agri and bioeconomy SMEs for green goals

January 13, 2026

The European Investment Bank announced a new intermediated loan with Piraeus Bank to on‑lend to SMEs and mid‑caps in Greece’s agriculture and bioeconomy sectors. At least 30% of the portfolio is dedicated to climate action and environmental sustainability. The facility is intended to improve access to long‑tenor, lower‑cost funding for small projects, including energy efficiency and other green upgrades. Greek SMEs can contact Piraeus Bank to explore eligibility for climate‑aligned financing under this EIB line.

EIB guarantee with Allianz Bank Bulgaria expands SME lending with green focus

January 14, 2026

The EIB detailed a guarantee operation with Allianz Bank Bulgaria to support new loans and leases for SMEs and mid‑caps across industry, agriculture and services. The portfolio includes a 25% Climate Action share to encourage projects that cut emissions and improve environmental performance. By reducing risk, the guarantee is expected to widen access to finance and improve terms for small businesses. Bulgarian SMEs planning energy efficiency or other green investments can approach Allianz Bank to apply.

EU deal trims CSRD scope and limits supplier data demands on small firms

January 15, 2026

A political agreement on the EU’s “Omnibus I” package raises CSRD thresholds and delays timelines, reducing how many companies must provide full sustainability reports now. Importantly for SMEs in large company supply chains, the deal caps the level of information that reporting companies can request from value‑chain partners with 1,000 or fewer employees. This should simplify ESG questionnaires and reduce documentation burden for small suppliers. SME owners supplying EU reporters can prepare a concise data set (for example, via VSME‑style disclosures) to meet the streamlined requests.
